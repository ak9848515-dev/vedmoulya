## Purpose

Goal Decomposition is the process of converting **high-level aspirations** into **executable actions**. It is the bridge between the human world of dreams and visions and the practical world of tasks and habits.

Without decomposition, goals remain abstract and unachievable. With decomposition, even the most ambitious vision becomes a series of manageable steps.

## Decomposition Levels

### Level 1: Vision → Long-term Goals

**Decomposition principle:** Extract the key domains of achievement from the vision.

**Input:** A vivid description of the desired future.

**Process:**

- Identify the major life domains the vision touches (career, business, skills, relationships, health)
- For each domain, define what success looks like
- Time-bound each domain outcome to 1-5 years
- Ensure each long-term goal independently contributes to the vision

**Output:** 3-7 long-term goals, each with a clear outcome and timeline.

### Level 2: Long-term Goals → Quarterly Goals

**Decomposition principle:** A long-term goal is too large to plan directly. Break it into quarterly chunks that each produce meaningful progress.

**Input:** A long-term goal with a 1-5 year horizon.

**Process:**

- What would meaningful progress look like in 3 months?
- What is the logical first quarter's work?
- What dependencies exist between quarters?
- What external factors might affect timing?

**Output:** 4-20 quarterly goals (one set per long-term goal, multiplied by quarters).

### Level 3: Quarterly Goals → Monthly Goals

**Decomposition principle:** A quarter is still too large for detailed planning. Monthly goals provide a natural rhythm for review and adjustment.

**Input:** A quarterly goal.

**Process:**

- Divide the quarterly work into monthly chunks
- Ensure each month delivers a mini-outcome
- Allow for natural variation in monthly capacity
- Account for holidays, events, and seasonal factors

**Output:** 3 monthly goals per quarterly goal.

### Level 4: Monthly Goals → Weekly Goals

**Decomposition principle:** A month is manageable but not actionable. Weekly goals translate into the rhythm of life.

**Input:** A monthly goal.

**Process:**

- What needs to happen each week to achieve the monthly goal?
- Identify natural breakpoints between weeks
- Ensure each week has a clear focus
- Balance difficulty across weeks

**Output:** 4-5 weekly goals per monthly goal.

### Level 5: Weekly Goals → Daily Plans

**Decomposition principle:** A week decomposes into days. Each day has a finite capacity. Daily plans are the bridge between goals and action.

**Input:** A weekly goal and current execution context.

**Process:**

- Distribute weekly work across available days
- Consider energy patterns (hard tasks on high-energy days)
- Consider external commitments and constraints
- Build in buffer time for unexpected events
- Prioritize — not everything can be done every day

**Output:** Daily plans for each day of the week, with prioritized tasks.

### Level 6: Daily Plans → Tasks

**Decomposition principle:** A day decomposes into tasks. Each task is a discrete, completable unit of work with a clear outcome.

**Input:** A daily plan.

**Process:**

- Define what completable work would constitute progress
- Each task should be completable in 30-90 minutes
- Tasks should have a clear definition of done
- Order tasks by priority and energy requirements
- Allow for task switching and breaks

**Output:** A prioritized list of tasks for the day.

### Level 7: Tasks → Micro Actions

**Decomposition principle:** Some tasks are still too large or ambiguous. Micro actions are the smallest possible unit of forward movement.

**Input:** A task that feels overwhelming or ambiguous.

**Process:**

- What is the absolute smallest first step?
- What would take less than 15 minutes?
- What removes the most uncertainty?
- What builds momentum?

**Output:** 2-5 micro actions per task, each completable in 5-15 minutes.

### Level 8: Micro Actions → Completed Outcomes

**Decomposition principle:** The only measure that matters is completion. Micro actions must lead to tangible, measurable outcomes.

**Input:** Micro actions.

**Process:**

- Complete each micro action
- Mark the task as done
- Capture any insights or learning
- Celebrate the win (small wins compound)

**Output:** Completed outcomes, tracked progress, captured learning.

## Decomposition Principles

| Principle                        | Description                                                                                |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Minimum viable decomposition** | Decompose only as much as needed to enable action. Over-decomposition is paralysis.        |
| **Natural breakpoints**          | Decompose at natural boundaries — end of quarter, end of week, end of day.                 |
| **Outcome-focused**              | Each level should produce a measurable outcome, not just activity.                         |
| **Balanced granularity**         | Long-term goals are broad. Daily tasks are specific. Each level has the right granularity. |
| **Dependency-aware**             | Decomposition respects dependencies — prerequisite work must come first.                   |
| **Capacity-aware**               | Each level must fit within the person's capacity at that level.                            |
| **Flexible**                     | Decomposition is not permanent. Tasks can be re-decomposed if too large or too small.      |
| **User-led**                     | The user can override any decomposition decision. The system suggests, the user decides.   |

## Decomposition Patterns

### The 80/20 Decomposition

Identify the 20% of work that will produce 80% of the outcome. Focus decomposition on that 20%.

### The Inversion Decomposition

Start from the completed outcome and work backward: What had to happen just before completion? And before that?

### The Analogy Decomposition

Use past similar goals as decomposition templates. What worked before for similar goals?

### The Dependency Decomposition

Identify the critical path of dependencies. Decompose along the dependency chain.

### The Time-Boxed Decomposition

Decompose based on available time rather than work required. "Given I have 2 hours, what can I complete?"

## Decomposition Quality

| Dimension         | Good Decomposition             | Poor Decomposition                             |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Granularity**   | Tasks are actionable           | Tasks are vague or overwhelming                |
| **Measurability** | Each outcome is measurable     | Outcomes are ambiguous                         |
| **Dependency**    | Dependencies are respected     | Tasks are blocked by uncompleted prerequisites |
| **Capacity**      | Fits available time and energy | Overwhelms available capacity                  |
| **Flexibility**   | Can be adjusted                | Rigid and brittle                              |
| **Clarity**       | Each level is clearly defined  | Levels bleed into each other                   |
