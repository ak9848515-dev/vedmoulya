## How to use this file

1. Fill a row only when you can name the person and say **why** you may contact them.
2. Do not write a "verified problem" from assumption. It is verified only when the
   person has said it in their own words — which means at earliest after the
   discovery call. Until then write `unverified — assumption`.
3. Move one row per stage, never skipping. Stage vocabulary:

   `blank → identified → permission confirmed → contacted (only with owner approval)
→ conversation → qualified / disqualified → proposal → accepted / lost`

4. **You are not allowed to move a row past `identified` without the owner approving
   the exact message first** ([`OUTREACH-DRAFTS.md`](./OUTREACH-DRAFTS.md)).
5. Every change is dated. A row with no date is a rumour.

## Column definitions

| Column                        | What goes in it                                                                                                                    | What must never go in it                                     |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Name / role**               | The real person and their job, e.g. `A. Surname — content lead at 6-person studio`                                                 | A company alone, "the marketing manager", a job-board handle |
| **Business type**             | One of: freelance writer · content lead in-house · fractional CMO · 1–5 person studio · other (say what)                           | "Agency" as a catch-all                                      |
| **Verified problem**          | Their own words from a real exchange, e.g. `"I thought I'd been paid for that one — client says they did"`                         | Anything you inferred from their website                     |
| **Source of introduction**    | `ex-colleague`, `replied to my post on X`, `referred by [name]`, `asked me about Y`                                                | "LinkedIn search", "scraped list", "bought list"             |
| **Permission / lawful basis** | `existing relationship` · `inbound enquiry` · `public business contact where relevant outreach is acceptable` · `platform permits` | "It's public so it's fine" (public ≠ permitted)              |
| **Discovery status**          | `blank` → `scheduled [date]` → `held [date]` → `no-show` / `declined`                                                              | "interested" (that is not a status)                          |
| **Objections**                | Verbatim quote + date, or `none recorded`                                                                                          | Your summary of what you think they meant                    |
| **Next action**               | A dated action with an owner, e.g. `2026-11-03 — send approved follow-up`                                                          | "follow up later"                                            |
| **Notes**                     | Anything factual and checkable                                                                                                     | (nothing here justifies invention)                           |

## Hard rules

1. **No contact without approval.** Every message is drafted in
   [`OUTREACH-DRAFTS.md`](./OUTREACH-DRAFTS.md), approved by the owner, and sent
   by the operator by hand. The product sends nothing.
2. **No qualification by assumption.** Hard criteria require direct knowledge
   (`ACQUISITION-WORKFLOW.md` §1); a guess is a failed criterion, not a passed one.
3. **A row that fails a hard criterion is a `disqualified` row** — keep it, write
   the reason, do not delete it. Ten honest entries beat ten padded ones.
4. **Ten is a ceiling, not a target to fill.** Six real prospects is a good outcome;
   two real ones is a better outcome than ten invented ones.
5. A row older than 14 days with no next action is **stale**. Mark it `lost` with
   a reason or close it.
