## Rules that apply to all three drafts

- Replace **every** `[bracket]`. A message with a live bracket does not get sent.
- One specific, true reference to their work. If you have not read it, do not send.
- No links, no attachments, no calendar link in the first message.
- ≤ 120 words for the cold/warm note; shorter is better.
- Never claim: AI writes the article, the tool collects payments, PDF/Word export,
  a hosted account, clients you have, savings, rankings, or availability urgency.
- One ask per message. Never two.
- If they do not reply twice, stop. Do not re-send the same text from another address.

## Draft 1 — Concise introduction (warm-adjacent, one specific reference)

> **NOT SENT — REQUIRES OPERATOR APPROVAL**

```text
Subject: [Specific thing they published] → one question about invoicing

Hi [First name],

I read [specific piece — e.g. "your post on onboarding emails for B2B SaaS"] —
[one concrete sentence showing you actually read it, e.g. "the part about cutting
step 3 is the opposite of what most people do"].

Quick question: when a client pays you, how do you confirm the money landed before
you count it as income? I'm asking because I'm running a short pilot that puts the
invoice, the payment reference and a human review in one ledger — the revenue figure
only moves after someone checks the receipt.

Three sessions over three weeks, one article and one invoice set up. I'd be happy to
show you the 5-minute version if that's useful.

[Your name] · [what you do, in one line]
```

**What this draft does not do:** no price, no package, no claim, no ask for a call —
only permission to continue the conversation. The price comes after they answer.

## Draft 2 — Warm referral request (asking a mutual contact to introduce)

> **NOT SENT — REQUIRES OPERATOR APPROVAL**

```text
Subject: Quick ask — one introduction

Hi [Referrer first name],

You know how [client payments / invoicing / freelance admin] works better than most
people I know.

I'm looking for one or two people who bill several clients a month and have had the
"I thought that invoice was paid and it wasn't" moment. I'm running a short pilot that
fixes exactly that: the revenue number doesn't move until a human checks the payment
reference.

Would you be comfortable introducing me to someone like that — or telling me
honestly that you don't know anyone? Either answer is useful, and I won't nudge you
about it again.

[Your name]
```

**Guardrail:** send this only to someone you actually have a relationship with. An
introduction is sent by **them**, not by you — do not ask for a name you then contact
cold claiming a referral.

## Draft 3 — Post-discovery follow-up (the call already happened)

> **NOT SENT — REQUIRES OPERATOR APPROVAL**

```text
Subject: Re: [the call topic] — the one thing you asked about

Hi [First name],

Thanks for [Tuesday / the 20 minutes] — the useful part for me was when you said
"[their exact words about the problem]".

You asked [their exact question, e.g. "whether it double-counts if my bookkeeper
re-enters a payment"]. The answer: it doesn't — a repeated entry with the same key
returns the same payment row, and I can show you that live in about five minutes.

If you want, the next step is a 15-minute call where I show the ledger and you tell
me whether USD 1,500 for three sessions, 500 to book the dates and 1,000 on delivery,
is a yes, a no, or a not-now. No obligation either way — a no is a useful answer.

[Your name]
```

**Guardrail:** the quote in quotes must be their real sentence. If you cannot find it
in your notes, do not invent one — cut the sentence.
