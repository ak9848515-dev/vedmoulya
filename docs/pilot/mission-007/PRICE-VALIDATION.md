# Price validation — testing USD 1,500 and the 500 + 1,000 structure

**Status:** prepared, untested · **Created:** 2026-10-26 · **Owner:** founder/operator
**Offer:** A — Revenue Integrity Pilot · **Price under test:** **USD 1,500** (USD 500 on
written acceptance, USD 1,000 on Day-14 acceptance criteria)

> **The price is a hypothesis.** Not one person has been asked about it. Every table
> below starts empty, and **no row may be filled in from imagination, from "someone
> would obviously pay that", or from a compliment.**
>
> **Compliments are not purchase intent.** "That's a really neat idea", "I'd definitely
> use that", "sounds cheap for what it is" — none of these is evidence. Only the
> behaviours in §3 count.

## 2. Questions to ask in every discovery call

Ask these **after** the problem section, before describing the package. Never defend
the number in the same breath as asking it.

### Value and budget anchors (ask first, without naming a price)

1. "Roughly what does it cost you today — in time — to work out what you've actually
   been paid each month?"
2. "When that payment story went wrong, what did it cost you?" _(get their number)_
3. "Have you paid for anything to solve admin like this before — a bookkeeper, a VA,
   a tool? What did that cost?"
4. "What's your rough budget for fixing the invoicing side this quarter?" _(if they
   decline, that is data — record "declined")_

### Alternatives and switching

5. "What would you compare this to — an accountant, a spreadsheet, another tool?"
6. "What would make you not do this even if the price were zero?"

### The price itself

7. "Three sessions, one article, one invoice and one verified payment — I'm proposing
   **USD 1,500**, 500 to book the dates and 1,000 when it's delivered and checked.
   **Is that a yes, a no, or a not-now?**"
8. "What number were you expecting before I said that?" _(write the number verbatim)_
9. "What would have to be true for that to be an easy yes?"

### The payment structure specifically

10. "Does splitting it 500 / 1,000 work for you, or would you rather pay all of it at
    the end — or half and half?"
11. "How would you normally pay a supplier like this — bank transfer, card, an invoice
    your bookkeeper processes?"
12. "Would a 500 deposit need approval from someone else?" _(tests whether the person
    on the call can actually commit)_

## 3. What counts as evidence — and what does not

| Signal                                                                       | Counts as                          | Why                                                          |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| "That's a great idea, I'd use it"                                            | **Not evidence**                   | Compliment. Free to give.                                    |
| "Sounds cheap actually"                                                      | **Not evidence**                   | Opinion about your price, not their purchase.                |
| "Send me a proposal"                                                         | **Weak signal**                    | Polite exit is cheap; note it, do not count it as qualified. |
| "I'd pay that" with no next step                                             | **Weak signal** — hypothetical     | Record it as hypothetical interest, explicitly.              |
| Asking a technical/integration question ("does it handle partial payments?") | **Medium signal**                  | They are modelling use.                                      |
| Volunteering their own loss number unprompted                                | **Medium signal**                  | Pain is real; price may still not be.                        |
| Agreeing a specific session date                                             | **Strong signal**                  | Time is a real commitment.                                   |
| Asking who to address the invoice to                                         | **Strong signal**                  | Purchase mechanics.                                          |
| Paying the 500 deposit                                                       | **Only actual willingness to pay** | Nothing else proves it.                                      |

**Rule:** a price is "validated" only when **someone pays it** — or, at minimum, gives
written acceptance and pays the deposit. Everything before that is a hypothesis with
interesting data attached.

## 5. Reading the results (only once you have ≥ 3 conversations)

| Pattern across 3+ real conversations         | Reading                                    | Action                                                                |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Two or more agree in writing at 1,500        | Price plausible for this segment           | Keep 1,500; move to deposit test                                      |
| Most name a number well below (e.g. 400–700) | The scope is priced as "odd job"           | Either shrink scope or accept a lower pilot as validation             |
| Most hesitate on _trust_, not price          | Price is not the blocker — proof is        | Do not discount. Deliver one reference, or offer a smaller first step |
| Most do not recognise the problem            | Positioning is wrong, not price            | Revisit the target segment before touching the number                 |
| Nobody will talk about price at all          | The calls are not reaching decision-makers | Fix qualification (`PROSPECT-TRACKER.md`), not the price              |

**Never** lower the price because of one lukewarm reaction. Change it only when a
pattern across real conversations supports it — and record the reasoning in
[`../OFFER-COMPARISON.md`](../OFFER-COMPARISON.md) §4 (P1/P2).

## 7. Objections about price and the honest answers

| Their words                              | Honest answer                                                                                                                                                           | Never                                                |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| "That's expensive for one article."      | "You're not buying the article — you're buying the ledger setup and the habit of verifying money, plus the article that proves the loop works. 12–14 hours of my time." | Agreeing it is expensive and discounting on the spot |
| "ChatGPT can write an article for free." | "It can write a draft. It can't tell you which invoice was actually paid. The pilot is about the ledger, not the prose."                                                | Pretending AI is the differentiator                  |
| "Why do you want money upfront?"         | "500 books the dates and shows we're both serious; 1,000 only when the seven acceptance criteria are met."                                                              | Trimming the deposit to 0                            |
| "Can I pay you from what it saves me?"   | "I can't promise savings — I haven't measured any. So the price is fixed, not performance-based."                                                                       | Inventing a savings figure                           |
| "Send it to my accountant."              | "Happy to — but the decision needs your yes, and the sessions need your time."                                                                                          | Treating the accountant as the buyer                 |
| Silent on price                          | Record "declined to discuss budget". Silence is not agreement.                                                                                                          | Writing "budget seemed fine"                         |

## 8. What is still unknown

- Whether anyone will pay **anything** for this.
- Whether 1,500 is above, below, or at the market for this segment.
- Whether the 500/1,000 split helps or hurts (it might read as "half upfront for an
  unproven service" and be worse than 750/750 or pay-at-end).
- Whether "content marketers" are the right segment at all — the offer's core is
  invoicing integrity, which may better fit a different buyer.

None of these can be answered from this repository. They can only be answered by
real conversations, which require the owner's approval of a real message.
