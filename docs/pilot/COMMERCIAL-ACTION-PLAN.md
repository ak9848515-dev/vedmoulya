contact — an existing relationship, an inbound enquiry, a public business contact
where relevant outreach is acceptable, or a platform that permits it. No scraped
lists, no purchased data, no cold-blast tooling.

## Step 2 — Hold three discovery conversations

30 minutes each, using ACQUISITION-WORKFLOW §2. Structure, in one line each:

1. **Frame (2 min):** "This is 30 minutes — I want to understand how you handle
   invoices and payments, tell you what I do, and we'll both decide whether a
   three-week pilot makes sense. If it doesn't, I'll say so."
2. **Their month (8 min):** "Walk me through last month, invoice by invoice."
   Listen. Do not pitch.
3. **The pain, quantified (5 min):** "Have you ever had a payment you thought had
   landed but hadn't?" **Write their number down.**
4. **Demo (7 min):** show the sample article, then the ledger trace
   (`sample-deliverable/sample-ledger-trace.md`). Say the line: _the number does
   not move until a human checks the receipt._
5. **Fit and price (5 min):** three sessions, USD `[1,500]` as a proposal. Ask:
   "is that a yes, a no, or a not-now?"
6. **Next step (3 min):** book Session 1, or agree a dated follow-up, or close.

**Done when:** three calls have happened and each has a written outcome
(proposal / dated follow-up / no) plus the client's own words and their quantified
pain — recorded as an interaction in Client Ops the same day.

## Step 4 — Prepare one tailored proposal for manual review

1. Copy [`PROPOSAL-AND-SOW.md`](./PROPOSAL-AND-SOW.md).
2. Fill §1 from the discovery notes — their words, their number.
3. Confirm §3 matches what the product does. Cut anything that does not.
4. Fill dates, price, and the named operator.
5. Put it in front of the owner. **The owner approves before it is sent.**
6. Send it by hand from the operator's own mailbox; log the interaction.

**Done when:** one proposal exists, approved, sent by the owner, and logged — with
its status `proposal` in Client Ops. Not before.

## Step 5 — Agree scope, delivery and payment terms

Before any calendar hold is accepted:

- [ ] The client has accepted **in writing** ("accepted" by email is enough) — quote it in the deal record.
- [ ] Price, currency, dates and the seven acceptance criteria are all in that written exchange.
- [ ] Deposit amount and payment channel are agreed (product records; it does not collect).
- [ ] Dates are held on both calendars.
- [ ] Deal status: `negotiation` → `won` **on written acceptance**. `won` means "accepted pilot", not paid.
- [ ] The client has decided: AI drafting yes/no, and which provider (G2).
- [ ] The data-handling questions in [`CLIENT-DATA-HANDLING.md`](./CLIENT-DATA-HANDLING.md) §2 are answered.

**Done when:** a written acceptance exists and the deal record quotes it. No money
is expected yet — and none is counted.

## Step 6 — Deliver the pilot and record payment only after valid evidence

1. Run the operator readiness checks (pilot package §9) the day before Session 1.
   **If any fails, say so — do not improvise in front of the client.**
2. Deliver the three sessions per [`DELIVERY-TERMS.md`](./DELIVERY-TERMS.md) §1.
3. When money actually appears in the account, follow the manual verification
   procedure (§5 of the same document): record → confirm the revenue view still
   shows 0 → have a named human compare the reference → `reviewPayment`.
4. Quote revenue **only** from `getRevenueOverview().paidTotal` (verified only).
5. At close-out: check each acceptance criterion, record misses as misses, record
   what broke, and decide the workspace's fate in writing.

**Done when:** all seven criteria are checked (or misses recorded), the verified
payment — if any — is in the ledger, and the balance is either invoiced because
the criteria were met or renegotiated in writing.

## What this plan deliberately does not do

- It does not contact anyone, draft a prospect-specific email, or send anything.
- It does not convert a proposal, a `won` lead, or an accepted pilot into revenue.
- It does not promise AI writing, PDF/Word files, publishing, payment collection,
  hosting, or automation that the product does not have.
