### The differences that are real

1. **A sells a capability and a habit; B/C/D sell content volume.** The pilot's
   deliverable is one article _plus_ the ledger/verification workflow and a
   one-pager the client can repeat. B/C/D are content-production programmes with
   no ledger component at all.
2. **A is deliverable from the product as it exists today** (client → article →
   invoice → recorded → verified, proven in the Mission 005/006 sample run). B/C/D
   require sustained delivery over weeks, a content calendar, social output and
   monthly performance reporting — **none of which has been demonstrated or
   verified in this repository.**
3. **A has no ongoing obligation.** B/C/D create recurring obligations the operator
   has never met once.

## 3. Recommendation

**Lead with Offer A (USD 1,500 Revenue Integrity Pilot) and retire B/C/D from any
first conversation.**

Rationale, in order of weight:

1. **Only A is backed by evidence.** The Mission 005/006 sample run exercises every
   step of A end to end against real application services. B/C/D would commit the
   operator to outputs (multiple articles, social posts, monthly reporting) that
   have never been produced once.
2. **A is the only offer whose differentiator a prospect can _see_.** "The number
   does not move until a human checks the receipt" is demonstrable in five minutes.
   "3 blog posts" is not distinguishable from any freelancer.
3. **A is the smallest honest promise.** If the pilot underperforms, the damage is
   one article and 6 hours. If a 3,000/month retainer underperforms, the damage is
   a refund dispute and a broken reputation in a small market.
4. **A produces the asset B/C/D cannot be sold without:** a delivered engagement,
   a client who has run the loop themselves, and (only with written permission) a
   reference. B/C/D should not be offered until that exists.
5. **Price anchoring works in A's favour.** A pilot at 1,500 followed by a monthly
   retainer is a natural ladder. Selling a 3,000/month retainer first, with no
   delivery history, is the hardest sale in the set.

**What to do with B/C/D:** keep them in the repository as a _later_ roadmap, and
label them as unvalidated in any place a prospect might see them. Do not hand a
prospect `SERVICE_PACKAGES.md` or `PROPOSAL_TEMPLATE.md` as-is (see U3/U4).

## 4. Price decisions the operator still has to make

These are **not** decisions this document can make for you. Until they are made,
treat every number as provisional.

| P1 | Is USD 1,500 the right opening number? | 1,500 · 900 (variant in pilot §7) · 2,500 (the older Mission 004 hypothesis) | Reaction from the first three discovery calls; the number they repeat back without flinching |

| P3 | Is the pilot a _discount_ toward a retainer? | Flat 1,500 · 1,500 credited against month 1 | Whether buyers ask "and then what?" |
| P4 | Currency and collection channel | USD bank transfer only · add a processor | What the first prospect can actually pay with |
| P5 | Do B/C/D stay on the price list at all? | Keep, relabel as "later" · withdraw · reprice | Whether U1–U7 get resolved |

**Rule until P1–P4 are decided:** quote A as _"USD 1,500 — a proposal for this
pilot, not a published rate"_, exactly as the pilot package already says.

## 5. What must never be claimed for any of these offers

- Live AI writing the deliverable (the article is human-authored; AI output is an
  unverified draft and was **not** exercised in the sample run).
- PDF or DOCX delivery (`exportContent` returns `supported: false` with Markdown
  bytes for those formats — the sample run now probes this and refuses to write them).
- Payment collection or reconciliation (no processor, no webhook; a human compares
  the reference).
- Autonomous outreach, prospecting or publishing.
- A hosted account, SLA or uptime commitment.
- Any client, testimonial, case study or revenue figure — none exist.
